🔧 RCV vs. ACV — What Does That Actually Mean?

Replacement Cost Value (RCV) means the insurance company pays what it costs to fix or replace something with a brand-new version today — no deductions for age or wear. Actual Cash Value (ACV) means they pay what the item is worth TODAY, after subtracting depreciation for age and condition. Think of it like this: RCV is like getting a brand-new TV. ACV is like getting what someone would pay for your old TV at a garage sale.

Your Roof: Your roof is covered on Replacement Cost (RCV) — no ACV roof endorsement was found on this policy. That is good news. Here is a concrete example: If your roof is 15 years old and costs $40,000 to replace today, under RCV you would receive approximately $40,000 minus your deductible ($2,500 AOP or $6,044 hurricane, depending on the cause). Under ACV, depreciation of roughly 60–70% over 15 years would mean you receive only about $12,000–$16,000 — leaving you to pay $24,000 or more out of pocket. Since your roof is on RCV, you are protected from that scenario.

Your Contents (Coverage C): Your personal property IS on ACV. That means if your 5-year-old laptop cost $1,500 new, you might only receive $600 after depreciation. Multiply that across every item in your home and the gap can be enormous. Ask your agent about upgrading Coverage C to Replacement Cost.

🏠 Property Value vs. Rebuild Cost — These Are NOT the Same Thing

Your home's market value is what a buyer would pay for it — including the land. Your rebuild cost is what it would cost to tear down the rubble and reconstruct the structure from scratch. In Florida, land can be worth 30–50% of market value, so insuring to market value often means you are over- or under-insured on the structure. Insurance should cover the rebuild cost, not the sale price.

🌊 FEMA Flood Zone Alert — CRITICAL for Hudson, FL

Flood is ALWAYS excluded from your homeowners policy. This is not a gap specific to your carrier — it is true for every homeowners policy in the United States. Storm surge from a hurricane, rising water from heavy rain, or overflowing rivers are all flood events — and all excluded.

Hudson, FL (Pasco County) sits directly on the Gulf of Mexico. This is one of the highest storm surge risk areas in the entire state of Florida. FEMA flood maps show significant portions of coastal Pasco County in AE and VE flood zones. If your mortgage lender requires flood insurance (common in AE/VE zones), you are legally required to carry it. Even in an X zone, flooding is a real risk.

NFIP is a separate federal program — not a policy from Homeowners Choice. You purchase it through a licensed agent or directly through FEMA. Private flood insurance is also available and often cheaper.

Action Steps:

► Learn about NFIP coverage: floodsmart.gov
